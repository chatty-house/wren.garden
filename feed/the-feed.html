The Feed

Feed · 16 November 2026

The camera had been Gary's idea, back when Rosie was still waking twice a night and they were both so tired they kept leaving the front door unlocked. A modest thing, sixty quid off Amazon, with night vision and an app that sent alerts to his phone whenever it detected motion.

He set it up above the porch on a Sunday afternoon while Noor held the ladder. It pointed at the front path and caught, in its wide-angle lens, most of the drive and a narrow wedge of the street. He tested it by walking to the bins and back. On the app, he watched himself: a grey-green figure, his eyes two bright coins where the infrared caught them. He looked like something from a nature documentary. A fox, maybe, caught mid-trespass.

For three months the camera did what cameras do. It recorded the postman. It recorded Gary leaving for work at 6:40. It recorded Noor's mother arriving on Tuesdays and standing on the step for a full nine seconds before ringing the bell, which was its own small revelation. It recorded foxes, plural, who were using the recycling bin as a social venue. It recorded rain.

Then in November it recorded something else.

The alert came at 3:11am. Gary was already awake because Rosie was cutting a tooth and had been screaming since two. He was in the kitchen warming milk and he glanced at his phone out of habit. The preview thumbnail showed the path. He tapped it.

A figure was standing on the drive. Not walking. Standing. Facing the house, about four metres from the front door. The infrared made it look the way it made everything look: grey-green, featureless, the contrast blown out where the light hit hardest. The figure was still. The timestamp in the corner ticked over. 3:11. 3:12.

Gary watched for twenty seconds, then went to the front window and looked out through the gap in the curtains. The drive was empty.

He went back to the app. The figure was still there. 3:12. It hadn't moved. He refreshed the feed. The figure was still there. He closed the app and opened it again. The drive, on the screen, had a person on it. The drive, through the window, did not.

He checked the camera's stored footage the next morning. Between 3:11:04 and 3:14:22, the drive was empty. The stored recording showed nothing. The live feed had shown something. He couldn't explain this and after some consideration he decided not to try.

It happened again four nights later. An alert at 2:47am. The figure, same position, same stillness. He didn't go to the window this time. He watched on the phone until the feed went dark at 2:51, a brief interruption, and when it came back the figure was gone.

He told Noor. She asked if the camera was malfunctioning. He said probably. She asked if he wanted to replace it. He said he'd think about it.

He didn't replace it.

Over the following weeks, the figure appeared six more times, always between two and four in the morning. It never moved. It never approached the door. It stood in the same spot on the drive, the same four metres from the house, and faced the camera or the door or wherever the face was, because the infrared couldn't resolve features at that distance. It was just a shape. A person-shaped absence of detail.

Gary started checking the feed before bed. Then he started checking it when he woke in the night. Then he started waking in the night specifically to check it. He would lie in the dark next to Noor and Rosie, who had migrated permanently into their bed, and hold his phone below the covers and watch the empty drive. Waiting.

On the twelfth occurrence, at 3:33am on a Tuesday in January, the figure was standing closer to the house. Six feet from the door. He was sure of this because he could see the welcome mat in the frame, and the figure's feet were almost touching it. He watched without breathing. The timestamp said 3:33. Then 3:34. The figure was motionless.

He got out of bed. He walked downstairs. He did not go to the window. He went to the front door and opened it.

The porch light came on automatically.

The drive was empty. The street was empty. The air was cold and smelled like rain.

He stood there for a long time. Then he took out his phone and opened the app.

On the screen, the figure was standing directly behind him.

He did not turn around.
